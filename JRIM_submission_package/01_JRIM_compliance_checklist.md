# JRIM Author-Guidelines Compliance Checklist

Journal: **Journal of Research in Interactive Marketing** (Emerald Publishing)
Guidelines source: https://www.emeraldgrouppublishing.com/journal/jrim (verify against the live page at submission — Emerald updates limits periodically)
Submission system: ScholarOne — https://mc.manuscriptcentral.com/jrim

Apply this checklist to `JRIM_formatted_manuscript.docx` and the tables document
before submission. Items marked **⚠ verify** could not be checked automatically
and must be confirmed against the manuscript file.

---

## 1. Scope and positioning (most common desk-reject reason)

- [ ] **⚠ verify — Explicit interactive-marketing framing.** JRIM's editors have
  publicly flagged that many submissions never engage *interactivity* itself
  (see the 2025 JRIM editorial "Where's the interactive marketing in the
  interactive marketing literature?", doi: 10.1108/JRIM-03-2025-0135). The
  introduction and discussion must frame AI service encounters as **interactive
  marketing exchanges** (two-way, real-time, technology-mediated
  consumer–firm interaction), not only as "AI in services."
- [ ] **⚠ verify — JRIM conversation.** Cite and engage at least 4–6 recent JRIM
  articles (2022–2025, especially the AI-in-interactive-marketing special issue)
  so reviewers see the paper extends the journal's own conversation.

## 2. Manuscript length

- [ ] Total length within JRIM's stated maximum word count (check the journal
  homepage; most Emerald marketing journals cap at ~12,000 words).
  Emerald's count **includes** the structured abstract, references, and all text
  in tables/figures, **plus 280 words for every table and figure**.
- [ ] If over the limit: trim literature review redundancy and move robustness
  checks/scale items to supplementary material (Emerald allows online-only
  supplementary files).

## 3. Title and title page (separate file — double-anonymous review)

- [ ] Title is concise — Emerald recommends **no more than 8 words** where
  possible; informative, includes key constructs.
- [ ] Separate title-page file contains: full title; all author names,
  affiliations, ORCID iDs; corresponding author email; author biographies;
  acknowledgements; **funding statement**; **conflict-of-interest statement**;
  ethics approval details (identifying version).
- [ ] **Main manuscript file is fully anonymised**: no author names, no
  acknowledgements, no identifying ethics-committee name (use "[blinded for
  review]"), no self-citations phrased as "our previous work", file metadata
  scrubbed (Word → Inspect Document → remove personal information).

## 4. Structured abstract

- [ ] Maximum **250 words total**, under 4–7 Emerald sub-headings:
  - Purpose *(mandatory)*
  - Design/methodology/approach *(mandatory)*
  - Findings *(mandatory)*
  - Research limitations/implications *(if applicable)*
  - Practical implications *(if applicable)*
  - Social implications *(if applicable)*
  - Originality/value *(mandatory)*
- [ ] Abstract avoids citations and abbreviations; findings stated as
  associations, not causal claims (consistent with the cross-sectional caveat).

## 5. Keywords and article classification

- [ ] 4–12 keywords (Emerald maximum is **12**); include "interactive
  marketing", "artificial intelligence", and the focal constructs so the paper
  surfaces in JRIM-relevant searches.
- [ ] Article classification selected: **Research paper**.

## 6. Headings, structure, and writing

- [ ] Clear numbered hierarchy (1., 1.1, 1.1.1); concise section headings.
- [ ] Standard empirical structure: Introduction → Literature/Theory &
  Hypotheses → Methodology → Results → Discussion (theoretical contributions,
  managerial implications) → Limitations and future research → Conclusion.
- [ ] Hypotheses labelled H1…H5 consistently in text, tables and figures.
- [ ] **⚠ verify — causal language audit**: replace "effect of/impacts/drives"
  with "is associated with/predicts (model-based)" outside of hypothesis
  statements, per the cross-sectional restraint paragraph (statement 6 in the
  statements file).

## 7. Tables and figures

- [ ] Tables typed (not images), numbered with Roman or Arabic numerals
  consistently, each with a brief descriptive title and source note if needed.
- [ ] Placement markers in text ("Insert Table 1 here") or tables placed in
  text consistently — follow one convention.
- [ ] Figures supplied in high resolution (.eps/.tiff preferred, or
  editable-quality Word/PowerPoint objects).
- [ ] **⚠ FLAG — file naming**: the tables file is named
  `manuscript_IJEMR_tables.docx`. Before submission, confirm the tables carry
  **no formatting, numbering style, or header/footer referencing IJEMR or any
  other journal**, and rename the file (e.g. `JRIM_manuscript_tables.docx`).
  Residual traces of another journal's template are a known reviewer red flag
  (signals a recycled rejection).
- [ ] Standard PLS-SEM reporting set present (reviewers at JRIM expect all of
  these):
  - Sample profile table
  - Measurement model: loadings, Cronbach's α, CR, AVE
  - Discriminant validity: **HTMT** (Fornell–Larcker alone is no longer
    sufficient) 
  - Structural model: path coefficients, t-values/p-values, bias-corrected
    bootstrap CIs (≥5,000 resamples), f², R², Q²
  - Common method bias: Harman single factor **plus** a stronger test (full
    collinearity VIFs or marker variable)
  - Model fit: SRMR for the saturated/estimated model

## 8. References — Emerald Harvard style

- [ ] In-text: (Surname, year) / (Surname and Surname, year) /
  (Surname *et al.*, year) for 3+ authors; multiple cites alphabetical,
  separated by semicolons.
- [ ] List: alphabetical by surname; Emerald format, e.g.:
  > Surname, A.B. and Surname, C.D. (2025), "Title of article",
  > *Journal Name*, Vol. 12 No. 3, pp. 45-67, doi: 10.xxxx/xxxxx.
- [ ] Every in-text citation appears in the list and vice versa.
- [ ] DOIs included where available.
- [ ] The five updated 2025 references (statements file, item 8) inserted in
  Emerald Harvard format and cited at the appropriate points in the text.
- [ ] Preprints (PsyArXiv) are acceptable but mark them clearly as preprints;
  if a published version now exists, cite the published version instead.

## 9. Mandatory declarations (Emerald requirements)

All present in this package (`02_submission_statements.md`) — paste into the
manuscript/title page as indicated:

- [ ] Ethics approval & informed consent (Section 3.3) — **fill in committee
  name, approval number, date**
- [ ] Funding statement (title page + ScholarOne field)
- [ ] Conflict-of-interest statement (title page + ScholarOne field)
- [ ] Data availability statement (end of manuscript)
- [ ] Generative-AI use disclosure (Emerald policy: AI tools cannot be authors;
  any use must be declared — see drafted statement 5)

## 10. Submission-system items (ScholarOne)

- [ ] Originality declaration: not published, not under review elsewhere
  (**⚠ if this paper was previously submitted to IJEMR or another journal,
  that's fine — it just must not be under review there concurrently**).
- [ ] All co-authors registered with institutional emails and ORCID.
- [ ] Structured abstract, keywords, classification entered into ScholarOne
  fields exactly as in the manuscript.
- [ ] Cover letter: 1 page — fit with JRIM scope, the interactivity framing,
  contribution in two sentences, confirmation of ethics/originality.

## Top-5%-tier improvements (beyond compliance)

These are the moves that separate accepted JRIM papers from compliant-but-rejected ones:

1. **Lead with the interactivity mechanism.** Reframe the contribution as: AI
   service agents change the *interaction logic* of service encounters, and the
   moral-relational vs instrumental pathway comparison explains *which side of
   the interaction* (relational signalling vs functional performance) drives
   satisfaction. This makes H5 the headline, not an add-on.
2. **Sharpen the H5 claim discipline.** Use the exact wording in statement 7:
   pathway-level explained-variance comparison (≈21.4 vs ≈13.1 percentage
   points), framed as model-based explanatory comparison — never "dominance"
   or causal superiority. Verify both figures against the final PLS output and
   report the decomposition method (e.g. Shapley/relative weights or
   R²-decomposition) in a footnote so reviewers can reproduce it.
3. **Pre-empt the two standard PLS-SEM objections** — common method bias and
   discriminant validity — in the Methods, not in the response letter.
4. **Practical implications with named levers.** JRIM is practitioner-aware:
   give 3–4 concrete design implications (e.g. when to invest in empathic
   conversational design vs response accuracy/speed), each tied to a specific
   finding.
5. **Future-research agenda tied to interactivity.** Close with experimental
   and longitudinal designs that would establish causality — this converts the
   cross-sectional limitation into a research agenda rather than a weakness.
6. **Language polish pass.** Emerald desk editors flag inconsistent UK/US
   spelling; pick one (Emerald accepts either) and run a consistency check.
